Why Florence‑2 struggles on your data

It’s a generative VLM, not a dedicated detector/segmenter. Boxes/polygons come from text generation + post-processing, not from a detection head trained to optimize IoU/mAP the way YOLO/DETR-style models do.
Open-vocab detection is phrase-sensitive. Small wording changes (“trash” vs “plastic bottle” vs “litter”) can change outputs a lot. TACO has many object types + clutter, so the model often “describes” rather than tightly localizes.
Small objects + heavy clutter + occlusion. TACO images often have many tiny items, partial visibility, and overlapping waste. Florence tends to produce either (a) no boxes, (b) one big box, or (c) scattered boxes with weak alignment.
Segmentation via “referring expression” is a weak supervision signal. The segmentation task is basically “mask what this phrase refers to” without strong instance boundaries. In busy scenes, the phrase can match multiple regions → polygons become incomplete or wrong.
Label mismatch / ambiguity. Dataset categories are fine-grained (and sometimes subjective). Florence has to map them from language; it may pick a plausible caption label that doesn’t match the dataset taxonomy.
Resolution + resizing effects. Internally the processor resizes/normalizes images; thin objects (straws, wrappers) lose detail quickly, making accurate polygons hard.
